1️⃣ Put the printer into service mode

Before touching hardware, they connect the printer to a PC and force it into Canon service mode using a specific button sequence.

Why this matters

Normal users can’t reset the error in normal mode

Service mode allows access to internal maintenance functions

2️⃣ Open the printer body

They partially disassemble the printer:

Remove outer plastic covers

Lift the top frame

Access the base of the printer

This is the messy part
The ink absorber pads are deep inside, near the bottom.

3️⃣ Remove the ink absorber pads

Inside you’ll find:

Multiple sponge-like pads

Completely soaked with black and color ink

Technicians either:

Replace the pads with new ones (official method), or

Clean and reuse them (very common in practice)

4️⃣ Clean or replace the pads

If cleaning (most common for older models like MP280):

Pads are washed with warm water or cleaning solution

Squeezed repeatedly until water runs clear

Fully dried (very important—usually overnight)

Why drying matters:

Wet pads can cause ink overflow

Moisture can damage electronics

5️⃣ Clean the inside of the printer

They also:

Wipe pooled ink from the base

Clean ink tubes and surrounding plastic

Check for leaks or cracks

This prevents:

Ink dripping later

Smearing or electrical shorts

6️⃣ Reinstall pads and reassemble

Once dry:

Pads go back into their original positions

Printer is reassembled carefully

Screws and clips restored

7️⃣ Reset the waste ink counter

This step is critical.

Even with clean pads, the printer will NOT work unless:

The waste ink counter is reset to zero

Technicians do this using:

Canon service software, or

Service-mode button commands

This tells the printer:

“Pads are empty again—safe to operate”

8️⃣ Test print & cleaning cycle

Finally:

Power on

Run a test print

Run a light cleaning cycle

Check for leaks

If no error appears → job done ✅

Why Canon discourages DIY

Not because it’s impossible—but because:

Ink is very messy

Pads are hard to reach

Incorrect reset can brick the printer

Ink stains are permanent 😅

Honest technician truth

For MP280-series printers, many service centers:

Clean pads instead of replacing them

Reset counters

Charge mainly for labor, not parts

That’s why repair often costs more than buying a new printer.

Quick reality check

✔️ Technically fixable

✔️ Not a true hardware failure

❌ Canon doesn’t intend end users to service it

❌ Often not cost-effective
